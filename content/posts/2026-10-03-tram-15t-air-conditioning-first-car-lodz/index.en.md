Škoda 15T number 9239 was loaded onto a transporter at DPP's central workshops on Thursday and sent
to Łódź. It is the pilot vehicle for the retrofitting of air conditioning into the first series of
Prague's 15T trams — the single largest comfort upgrade the fleet has had since the cars entered
service, and one the city argued about for years before it was contracted.

The work goes to ENIKA, a Polish company that won the tender in July after a competition lasting
more than a year, and will be done in the halls of MPK Łódź, the Polish city's transport operator.

## What it costs and how far it goes

ENIKA's winning bid was 3.217 million crowns excluding VAT for one tram. Across the 123 cars covered
by the framework contract that comes to 395.685 million crowns excluding VAT, which DPP says is just
under 36 million below the estimated value of the contract. Three bidders competed.

The 123 cars are the whole first half of the 15T delivery, with two exceptions — numbers 9243 and
9285, which already have saloon air conditioning. Once the programme is running properly the pace is
to be at least twenty trams a year, scheduled around each vehicle's maintenance intervals. ENIKA
says that in full flow a tram will travel from Prague to Łódź roughly every fortnight, with a
completed car coming back the other way.

## Three units, four modes, five years of warranty

Each tram gets three separate air-conditioning units, one per articulated section. Each has 17 kW of
cooling and can also heat at 6 kW. Operation is automatic, run by the units' own control system in
four working modes: cooling, ventilation, ventilation with preheating of the intake air at low
temperatures, and tempering the vehicle. The units use an environmentally friendly refrigerant
exclusively, and carry a sixty-month warranty.

ENIKA has done this before. The company retrofitted air conditioning into Bombardier NGT6 trams for
the transport operator in Kraków.

## The pilot is a gate, not a formality

Nothing else leaves Prague until 9239 comes back. The prototype faces homologation and trial
operation on its return, and only after the technology has been successfully verified will DPP start
sending further trams to Poland. That sequencing is the reason this is a story about a single tram
rather than about a contract signed in July: until the first conversion is type-approved, the
395-million programme exists on paper.

It is also the right way round. Three units cutting into the roof of an articulated tram is a
structural and electrical intervention on a vehicle that has fifteen years of service behind it and
more ahead. Discovering a problem on car one is cheap. Discovering it on car thirty, after six
months of fortnightly shuttles to Łódź, is not.

## Why air conditioning became the argument it did

The 15T is Prague's standard modern tram and the first series arrived without saloon cooling, which
is now the comfort feature passengers notice before any other. That omission is what makes this
retrofit worth 396 million crowns rather than being left to the next generation of vehicles: the
cars concerned have most of their working lives still to run, and the alternative to converting them
is passengers riding un-air-conditioned trams into the 2040s.

The same question sits underneath the dispute now running between DPP and ROPID over whether to
rebuild more T3R.PLF cars, where the organiser's stated preference is new trams or reconstructions
that add full-vehicle air conditioning. Read together, the two files are one argument about how much
of Prague's existing fleet is worth upgrading rather than replacing — and this is the half of it that
has a signed price.
