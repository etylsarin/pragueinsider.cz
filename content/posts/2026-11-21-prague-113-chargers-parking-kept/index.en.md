Prague's city council has approved the siting of another 113 slow charging points for electric cars,
spread across four boroughs: Praha 12, 13, 15 and 17. The resolution was passed on 16 November,
Zdopravy.cz reported. It came with a condition the boroughs had fought for. The parking spaces next to
the new chargers will not be reserved for cars while they charge; anyone may park there, including
cars with combustion engines.

## A no that became a yes

The four district town halls first opposed the locations City Hall had proposed. At a joint meeting
on 10 November they agreed to them, on the understanding that existing parking would be kept as it
is. According to the explanatory report to the council resolution, cited by Zdopravy.cz, the European
grant that pays for most of the programme does not require the spaces to be reserved. Dedicated
charging bays will therefore appear only where a borough asks for them itself.

The split is uneven. Praha 13 gets the most, 40; Praha 12 gets 39, Praha 15 19 and Praha 17 15. Among
the streets named are Bellušova, Kettnerova, Pejevové, Milerova, Boloňská, Milánská and Skuteckého.
PREdistribuce has checked each site for a grid connection, and IPR Praha, the city's planning
institute, has issued a favourable opinion. The city company Technologie hlavního města Prahy (THMP)
is working on the preparation.

What the council approved is where the chargers go, not when they work. At the start of November,
City Hall said the project was still at an early stage of design, according to the same report.

## Part of 1,500

The 113 belong to a larger programme approved in January this year: up to 1,500 public chargers of
up to 22 kW across the city. Its estimated cost is about 493 million crowns, and the EU grant may
cover up to 80 per cent of eligible costs. Construction is to be shared between THMP and the
Technical Administration of Roads (TSK), and the city companies have previously said the whole
programme should be finished in mid-2028.

## Why it matters

A charger on a residential street is a claim on the kerb, and on most residential streets the kerb
is parking. The boroughs' objection was not to charging but to losing
spaces to it, and they won the point: the posts will go in, and the bays beside them stay general
parking unless a borough decides otherwise.

That leaves an open question the source does not answer. A charger next to a space any car may
occupy is only usable when that space happens to be free, or held by an electric car. How often that
is will depend on how full those streets are at night. Read this way, the agreement trades
reliability of charging for the parking residents already have, and it lets each borough choose its
own balance. Since the 1,500 will need sites in many more districts, the terms agreed with these four
are likely to be the first thing the next ones ask for — that is our reading, not something the
report states.
