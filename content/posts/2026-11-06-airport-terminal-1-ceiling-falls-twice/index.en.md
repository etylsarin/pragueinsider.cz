Twice within a few weeks, part of the suspended ceiling in Terminal 1 at Václav Havel Airport has
fallen, and both times in the place where the building is being worked on: the arrivals passport
control, which has been under reconstruction since September. Letiště Praha confirmed both incidents
to Zdopravy.cz on 5 November. No injuries are reported in either.

The first came to light only because of a video. Security-camera footage posted on X shows a ceiling
section carrying display screens coming down directly in front of the passport desks — the space
where arriving passengers stand in the queue, and through which, Zdopravy.cz notes, thousands of
people pass every day. The airport said the clip was not new.

> The attached video does not show today's event but an incident from October. These are therefore
> two separate events in the course of the ongoing reconstruction connected with the expansion of
> the Terminal 1 passport control.
>
> — Letiště Praha, statement to Zdopravy.cz

The second fall happened at around 04:30 on 5 November. According to the airport's spokesperson,
Denisa Hejtmánková, the affected area had already been closed off and cleared as a precaution because
of the works, so nobody was in it. The airport says the incident has had no effect on check-in or on
passenger movement, and the damage has not yet been put at a figure.

## Whose fault, by the airport's account

On the October fall, the airport's preliminary conclusion is that it was caused by a mistake on the
part of the building contractor. The contractor then assessed the situation and decided that work
would continue. The airport has given no cause for the second fall.

Zdopravy.cz does not name the contractor in its report. The construction contract for the passport
control, as the same outlet reported from the public contracts register in September, went to a
consortium of BAK stavební společnost and PULSKLIMA for CZK 259.8 million excluding VAT. Whether the
ceiling work was theirs or a subcontractor's is not stated.

The person who posted the October video accused the airport's management of keeping both incidents
quiet. The airport rejected what it called unfounded claims of deliberate concealment. It is worth
noting what is on the record: the October fall became public through a video posted on social media, not
an airport announcement, and the airport's confirmation of both came on the day the clip circulated.

## The works go on

The project is the one this site covered when it started: five more conventional counters and four
EasyGoGate self-service gates in Terminal 1 arrivals, a larger waiting area in front of the border
control and a complete renewal of the ventilation. According to the airport, the air-handling
reconstruction began this September and is being carried out with the terminal in full operation.
The September contract notice put the noisy, dusty and demolition work mostly between 22:00 and
05:00. The second fall, at 04:30, came inside that window; the airport has not said when in October
the first one happened.

Completion is still expected at the end of April 2027. Nothing in the airport's statement suggests
the schedule or the method has changed after the second incident.

That is the open question. Building inside a terminal that never closes is the premise of the whole
job, and the airport's answer to two falls is that the area was cordoned and the hours were quiet.
That held on 5 November. The October footage, though, shows the ceiling coming down on the queuing area
itself, and the airport has said nothing yet about what, if anything, changes over the passport desks
for the five months of work still to come.
