Prague Airport is replacing the systems behind every check-in desk it has. The contract, worth 195
million crowns, has gone to SITA, which will also provide service and support for the six years the
contract runs. The airport plans to switch the new platform on in April 2027. Airport spokeswoman
Denisa Hejtmánková gave the figures to Zdopravy.cz, which reported them on 7 October.

What is being changed is the layer passengers never see and feel immediately when it fails. The
modernisation covers software and hardware at all check-in and transfer desks and at the departure
gates: computers, monitors, keyboards, the readers for travel documents and boarding passes, and the
printers that produce baggage tags. The tender documentation anticipates the delivery of 450
computer terminals.

## The self-service half

The contract also replaces the generation of self-service check-in kiosks, the machines that let a
passenger check in without going to a desk. Thirty-five of them are to be delivered.

Self-service baggage drop changes too, and here the two terminals move in opposite directions. At
Terminal 2 the airport is replacing the twelve oldest units, in operation since 2020, with newer SITA
Scan&Fly Mini machines of a type already used at both terminals. At Terminal 1 the count goes up
rather than over: eight further units of the same type are being added, so more passengers there can
hand over a bag without queueing at a staffed desk.

SITA already supplies the check-in platforms at Prague Airport, so this is a continuation rather
than a change of supplier. The new contract also pulls service, support and the further development
of self-service baggage handling together under one arrangement, which the airport presents as part
of the point.

## Why a back-office contract is a building story

Nothing in this contract moves a wall, and that is what makes it worth writing down. Terminal
capacity can be raised two ways. One is construction, which is what most airport stories are about
and what the building site at Prague has been delivering for years. The other is throughput at the
desks and kiosks already standing — which is what 450 terminals, 35 kiosks and eight extra bag drops
at Terminal 1 buy.

The second kind is cheaper by an order of magnitude and arrives faster. It is also the kind that
decides how a terminal feels at six in the morning, when the constraint is not floor area but how
many people can be processed per minute and how many of them can do it themselves. A six-year
service contract signed with the incumbent supplier is not a dramatic document. It is, for the next
six years, the thing standing between a full terminal and a queue out of the door.

One figure is worth holding the airport to. Zdopravy.cz reports the modernisation as due for
completion next year, with the launch planned for April 2027. Replacing 450 terminals and the
software behind them across two live terminals without a day of disruption is the kind of promise
that is easy to make eighteen months out.
