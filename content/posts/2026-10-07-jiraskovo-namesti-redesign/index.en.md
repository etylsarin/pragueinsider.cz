Jiráskovo náměstí is to be rebuilt, and the stated reason is the behaviour of the people standing on
it. Prague Morning reported on 6 October that Praha 2 intends to carry out a redesign of the square
over the next two years at an estimated cost of around CZK 35 million, replacing part of the lawn
with paving, restoring a historic fountain and relocating the monument to Alois Jirásek. The scheme
follows a 2022 study by the archiF studio. Praha 2's own pages are not reachable from here, so every
figure below rests on Prague Morning's account.

## The damage is the brief

The square sits directly opposite the Dancing House, which makes it the standing place for the
building's photograph. Visitors step onto the grass to get the frame, and one of the commonest poses
has them appearing to prop up or kick the building with a foot. The grass has been heavily damaged by
the repeated footfall and attempts to restore it have not held.

The response is to stop defending the ground and pave it. Part of the lawn becomes a durable paved
surface that takes the photographers, which is intended to relieve the pressure on the greenery that
remains. A raised edge around the lawn doubles as seating. The northern part of the square gets new
flowerbeds and trees, and shrubs judged unsuitable for the position are removed. The Jirásek
monument — the square carries the writer's name — moves to the centre of the small park.

Paving over a lawn is the kind of decision that reads as a loss until you look at what is being
lost. There is no version of this square in which a few thousand people a week stand somewhere else;
the only choice was between grass that is reliably bare and a surface designed for the use the place
actually gets.

## Hofman's fountain comes back

The second half of the project is restoration rather than management. The square holds a Cubist
fountain by the architect Vlastislav Hofman. Under the scheme the ground around it is repaved and the
fountain itself is returned to its original appearance and to working order.

What makes that possible is archival. Soňa Formanová, an architect and one of the project's authors,
said the team located Hofman's original documentation, including drawings covering the fountain and
the approaches to the nearby bridge. A restoration carried out from the author's own drawings is a
different proposition from one carried out from photographs, and it is the detail in Prague Morning's
account most worth holding onto: it means the fountain can be put back as designed rather than as
remembered.

It also quietly widens the project. Hofman's drawings for the bridge approaches describe ground
beyond the fountain's basin, which is to say the square was designed once, as a composition, by the
same hand. Nothing in the report says Praha 2 intends to follow those drawings any further than the
fountain. But a district that has found them will be asked about the rest.

## What is not yet known

The reporting gives a budget, a two-year horizon and a design origin four years back, and that is the
extent of it. There is no procurement stage, no contractor, no start date, no named district official
and no breakdown of the CZK 35 million between paving, fountain and planting. A 2022 study reaching
implementation in 2026 and 2027 is a normal pace for a district square, not a fast one, and the gap
is long enough that the costing deserves a second look when the district publishes it.
