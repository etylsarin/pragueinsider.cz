In September 2020 the architecture centre CAMP filled its gallery with the projects that were
supposed to remake Prague. Six years later it has published a status check on all of them, and the
list has quietly sorted itself into two categories: things now under construction, and things still
being studied.

## Under the ground, mostly on schedule

Metro line D is the clearest example of the first group. Tunnelling is under way on the
Pankrác–Ryšánka section, with the Pankrác–Olbrachtova stretch starting this month and
Olbrachtova–Nové Dvory following two years later. The line runs 10.6 kilometres with nine regular
stations and one interchange, and will operate fully automatically without drivers. Hochtief and
Subterra were selected in April for the Ryšánka–Tempo section. The opening of Pankrác–Depo Písnice
is planned for December 2027, with the full line including Náměstí Míru–Pankrác at the end of 2029.

The airport rail link is the other large civil-engineering commitment, targeted for completion in
2030 and part-funded through the EU's Connecting Europe Facility. It is being built in overlapping
segments: Masarykovo nádraží expands from seven tracks to nine by 2027, Bubny–Výstaviště completes
this year, Veleslavín–Ruzyně in 2028, and the Výstaviště–Dejvice, Dejvice–Veleslavín and
Ruzyně–airport sections in 2029. The finished route adds six stations, runs fully electrified and
double-tracked, and includes a transfer terminal at Dlouhá Míle with over a thousand park-and-ride
spaces.

The city ring road remains the long tail. Documentation work on the 10.5-kilometre completion —
Pelc-Tyrolka–Balabenka, the Štěrboholská radiála and the Libeňská spojka, largely in tunnel — began
in November 2025, with a permit application expected in early 2027 and opening projected for the
second half of the 2030s.

## Two river crossings, already done

Dvorecký most opened ceremonially in April: 331.6 metres, six spans, prestressed concrete, carrying
trams, pedestrians and cyclists but no cars, and set a metre above the 2002 flood level. It was
designed by Tubes Atelier and Atelier 6 at a cost of around a billion crowns, with a sculptural
garden by Kryštof Kintera. The Troja footbridge, replacing the structure that collapsed in 2017, is
also complete.

## The squares are where residents will notice

Wenceslas Square is half finished: the lower section is done and the upper section is a building
site, with the new tram line from Jindřišská to Vinohradská expected to carry its first passengers
in June 2027.

Karlovo náměstí — at over 70,000 square metres the largest square in the country — is the bigger
undertaking. Dendrological work and tree relocation ran from autumn 2025 through spring 2026 at
roughly nine million crowns; the main revitalisation begins this year and must finish by November
2030 to satisfy its EU funding. The roughly 250-million-crown design by Rehwaldt
Landschaftsarchitekten with BY Architects and PD Filip rebuilds the path network and adds a café, a
children's playground, an outdoor exhibition space and a market area by the New Town Hall. The
stated aim is prosaic and telling: a comfortable route across the square, particularly from the
metro toward the hospital.

Also moving: the Hradební korzo, a continuous circuit tracing the former fortification line from
Národní třída through Revoluční, with Aoc architekti holding creative authority alongside
Metroprojekt. Reconstruction of Revoluční is planned to start in 2027.

## Still on paper

The brownfields tell the other half of the story. Smíchov is the exception that proves it —
genuinely under construction, with around 1,600 apartments across two sections, a Česká spořitelna
headquarters in the southern half, a kilometre-long pedestrian spine linking them, terminal
completion at the end of 2028 and the public transport terminal by 2030.

Elsewhere it is studies. Rohan City has a Daniel Libeskind visualisation. Bubny-Zátory is still
completing its territorial study. Bohdalec-Slatiny finished a pre-verification study by Jakub
Cigler Architekti in February 2025 and has a territorial study tender in preparation. Nákladové
nádraží Žižkov is the middle case: buildings visualised, and a tram line with five new stops
running through the district to Jarov.

Holešovická tržnice is on a transformation running to 2038 with total investment around five
billion crowns; the Burza building restoration finished in 2025. The Vltava Philharmonic expects to
file for a building permit this summer.

Six years produced two opened bridges, one metro line under construction, an airport railway in
pieces across the city, and a set of brownfields that are better documented than they were. Whether
that reads as progress depends entirely on which half of the list you were waiting for.
