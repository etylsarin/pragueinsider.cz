Building a fourth track on the railway between Běchovice and Libeň will close the bridge on
Broumarská street in Kyje for seven months, splitting the neighbourhood in two. That was the news
residents took away from a meeting in Kyje on Wednesday, where representatives of Správa železnic
and the project's designers presented the scheme, Zdopravy.cz reported on 15 November. Detours will
be difficult; for people on foot, at least a temporary footbridge is to be built.

The bridge is not the only cost. While the fourth track is built, suburban trains will probably not
stop at Kyje or Dolní Počernice at all: for safety reasons it will not be possible to run on the
neighbouring third track either. The designers are already working through this with ROPID, the
regional transport authority. Temporary platforms are still among the options being considered,
but according to Zdopravy.cz that variant is probably not very realistic.

## What is being built

The Běchovice–Libeň section, via Dolní Počernice, is to form the northern arm of the scheme called
VRT Praha, the entry of the planned high-speed lines into the capital from the east. Despite the
name it will not be a high-speed railway, which Czech law defines as running above 200 km/h. After
the rebuild it remains a conventional line for up to 160 km/h, and through Kyje somewhat slower
than that.

The gain is capacity. A fourth track is added to the existing three on the south side, which lets
dispatchers keep stopping regional trains on the outer tracks and concentrate freight and
long-distance traffic on the inner two. The railway's protective zone widens by only four metres.
Noise barriers move with it, underpasses and bridges over the line are lengthened, and lifts are
added at the stops to make them step-free.

The existing track itself will not be touched during construction; only the signalling changes.

> The signals will be removed, and ETCS L2 with fixed blocks will be installed on the section.
> Signals will probably remain only at Libeň.
>
> — Michal Babič, designer, as quoted by Zdopravy.cz

The fourth track will begin before Běchovice, roughly where the Klánovice forest ends; the
designers said starting it as far out as Úvaly was not possible because the forest is protected.
The section now being prepared, however, starts at Běchovice station, and that is where construction
is to begin in 2029.

## Where the scheme stands

The fourth track has been designed to the level of documentation for the zoning decision, and Lukáš
Tittl, deputy director of Správa železnic's western construction directorate, was able to show the
meeting visualisations of the widened line. In October Správa železnic tendered the next stage, the
documentation for the construction permit and for execution. Design work will run through next
year, the EIA opinion will be obtained, and the first negotiations with owners of affected land
will begin. Construction is now expected to start in the second half of 2029 and to finish in 2033.

## The flyover that was meant to come first

One date has moved. The Libeňský přesmyk, a flyover nearby that mainly helps freight trains pass
through Libeň station, should already have been finished under the original plans. Zdopravy.cz now
reports that it will probably be built at the same time as the northern branch instead. In the
second half of the 2030s it is to be joined by the Jahodnická spojka.

That matters for the order of works. When Správa železnic launched the design tender in October, the
project's documentation assumed the přesmyk would already be in service by the time work on the
fourth track began, and the choice of the south side for the new track was partly justified by it.
Building the two together means Libeň and the corridor east of it would be under construction at
once. The report does not say whether that changes the closures announced for Kyje.

## Two branches, one after the other

VRT Praha also has a southern branch, from Běchovice to Zahradní Město and on into the centre. Two
branches are being built so that high-speed trains from every direction can run straight through
the main station. They will not be built at the same time, so that the railway can stay in
operation throughout; the northern branch comes first in the schedule.

Read with the earlier reporting, the picture is of a scheme that has stopped being a line on a map.
What residents of Kyje heard on Wednesday is what it will mean for them in practice: a bridge, a
train stop and several years of works, with the decisive environmental assessment still to come.
