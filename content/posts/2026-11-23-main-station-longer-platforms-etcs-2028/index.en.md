Správa železnic, the state rail infrastructure manager, is preparing another large round of work at
Prague's main station for 2028 and 2029. The project, filed as a *cyclic renewal* of worn-out
infrastructure, will install the ETCS train control system at the station and lengthen four of its
platforms. According to the document approved by the authority's central commission, reported by
Zdopravy.cz on 23 November, the estimated investment cost is CZK 1.76 billion excluding VAT.

The approval itself is routine — the central commission is the stage every large Správa železnic
project has to clear. What is not routine is the timing. It lands six days after two commuter
trains collided head-on just outside the station, at a junction with no ETCS, and the authority is
now under instruction to see whether it can be brought forward.

## Longer platforms on the northern side

The platform work concerns platforms 1 to 4. Nela Friebová, spokeswoman for Správa železnic, told
Zdopravy.cz:

> Specifically, they will be lengthened at the northern throat by up to 79 metres, with the extent
> differing between individual platforms. The change will allow longer trains to be handled more
> efficiently and the station's capacity to be better used. The main station will gain a larger
> reserve for growing regional and long-distance traffic.
>
> — Nela Friebová, spokeswoman, Správa železnic

The bulk of the renewal is at the opposite end. At the southern throat — the fan of tracks towards
the Vinohrady tunnels — every set of points and every switch assembly is to be replaced. At the
northern throat, one crossing switch will be renewed.

The commission document makes the case in terms of reliability rather than capacity: the work is
meant to head off the faults that currently disrupt normal operation, whether through frequent,
hard-to-plan closures or through restrictions caused by technical failures, and to remove the need
for the speed limits those problems bring.

## ETCS, but the lower level

The station will get ETCS Level 1 LS, together with a warning system for a train passing a signal
at danger without authority, and repeater signals.

That is a step below what most of the Czech network is getting. Elsewhere the standard is ETCS
Level 2, which sends data to the train continuously over the GSM-R radio network. At the main
station that is not possible, Friebová said, because the existing GSM-R network lacks the capacity
to support Level 2 on the scale needed. The station will instead use Level 1, which passes
information to the train only at fixed points, through balises laid in the track.

## The question of sooner

On Friday, according to Zdopravy.cz, Správa železnic was given the task at a safety commission of
examining whether the work could begin earlier — chiefly, the report says, because of Tuesday's
collision and the ETCS installation.

That collision, on 17 November, injured 46 people when a CityElefant leaving for
Milovice and an inbound RegioFox met on the bridge over Seifertova at U Bulhara. The rail
inspectorate's first view was a signal passed at danger, and Zdopravy.cz reported at the time that
ETCS would most likely have prevented it (we covered it on 18 November).

Read together, the two reports explain what the plan does and does not promise. The signal-passing
warning in the project is precisely the protection that was missing last Tuesday, and it is
currently scheduled to arrive in 2028 at the earliest. Whether the safety commission's request
moves that date is the thing to watch; the report gives no indication yet of what an earlier start
would look like or what it would cost.
