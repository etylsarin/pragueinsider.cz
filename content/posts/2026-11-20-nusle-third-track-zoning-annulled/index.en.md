The Ministry of Transport has annulled the zoning decision for Správa železnic's plan to run three
tracks instead of two on the line leaving Prague's main station towards Vršovice, and has ordered
Prague City Hall to hear the case again. The decision was issued by the city in August 2023. The
ministry's ruling, published this week on its official notice board and reported by Zdopravy.cz on
20 November, means the permit the railway was counting on no longer exists — and, according to the
same report, Správa železnic now does not expect construction to begin before 2032.

## How it got here

The ministry is not the first body to find fault. The decision had already been overturned by the
Regional Court in Ostrava and by the Supreme Administrative Court. According to Zdopravy.cz, the
courts identified problems including the way the noise burden was described and the fact that the
binding opinions of the public-health authorities could not be reviewed.

The challenge came from the ground. Local residents and landowners first appealed to the ministry
and lost; only then did they go to court. Their objections, as Zdopravy.cz summarises them, were
that the effects on the plots concerned were unclear and that the environmental impact assessment
(EIA) was out of date. The zoning documentation was prepared for Správa železnic by Sudop Praha.

Read in sequence, the ministry is now doing what its own appeal ruling did not: sending the file
back to the city with the defects the courts named still to be put right. That is our reading of
the order of events in the report, not something the source spells out.

## What the third track is for

The scheme is about capacity, and it is achieved by moving a set of points. Today the switches sit
roughly beneath the Nusle Bridge. Správa železnic wants to move them several hundred metres towards
the main station, to the level of Na Folimance street, so that trains in and out of the station can
use three tracks over a longer stretch. The third track runs to and from Vršovice.

The railway has also promised the neighbours something in return, in a statement Zdopravy.cz
quotes from earlier:

> Besides higher capacity, the benefit will also be an increase in line speed to 60–70 km/h.
> Anti-vibration mats and other elements will be used along the whole length to reduce vibration
> and noise in this densely built-up area. The builders will also widen several underpasses.
>
> — Správa železnic, as quoted by Zdopravy.cz

## Why it matters

The stretch under the Nusle Bridge carries trains in and out of the main station, and Správa
železnic itself calls the area it runs through densely built-up. That combination is why it is contested: the
same capacity that the railway needs is carried past windows in Nusle and Vršovice. Two courts and
now the ministry have agreed with the residents that the paperwork did not show its working on
noise and health. A new hearing does not kill the scheme, but it resets the clock on it, and the
2032 date is the railway's own estimate of what that costs.
