Prague 6 councillors have decided to file a cassation complaint against the Municipal Court ruling
that quashed the borough's morning one-way restriction on Na Krutci. The aim, the borough says, is
less to win back one street than to get a ruling from the Supreme Administrative Court (Nejvyšší
správní soud) that districts can use as a guide whenever they try to keep through-traffic out of
residential areas. Both the ruling and the appeal were reported by Zdopravy.cz, the second on the
basis of a Prague 6 press release.

## What the court threw out

The measure, issued as a public notice dated 9 June 2026, closed Na Krutci on weekdays between
7 and 9 a.m. to non-residents heading towards Veleslavín and Evropská. It was a temporary traffic
arrangement, introduced on a trial basis and due to run only to the end of the year.

Na Krutci is the shortcut drivers use to escape the queue on Horoměřická, which builds every
morning in part because a bus lane there is still missing and keeps being postponed. Residents of
Vokovice complained that commuters from Central Bohemia filled their streets and left them unable
to get out onto Evropská themselves. Once the street was made one-way, the commuters from Nebušice
and Horoměřice organised a petition of their own and went to court. The Central Bohemian Region
filed a similar suit, arguing that suburban bus lines 312, 316 and 356 were being held in the
queue and that delays risked pushing passengers back into cars.

The court refused an interim injunction in September, finding no concrete and imminent serious
harm. Ruling on the merits in November, it found the measure unlawful and ordered it void from the
day the judgment took legal effect. "The choice of a temporary traffic arrangement on the roads
concerned was not sufficiently and convincingly justified," the judgment says, according to
Zdopravy.cz, which also quotes the court's broader point: a legitimate aim does not relieve the
authority of the duty to justify its chosen form of regulation on properly established facts, and
to weigh local benefits against the other public interests affected. The court found no such
reasoning in the notice and declared it unreviewable.

## What Prague 6 wants decided

The borough says it wants the higher court to clarify three things: what a traffic measure's
justification has to contain, how far its effects must be assessed beforehand, and whether a trial
period can be used before a permanent arrangement is introduced. It also disputes how the court
weighed the evidence, above all the supporting study from ČVUT.

> "This cassation complaint is meant to clarify which legal tools Prague 6 can use."
>
> — Petr Prokop, mayor of Prague 6

Ondřej Matěj Hrubeš, the councillor for transport, argues that the measure calmed traffic in
Vokovice and that the queues it was blamed for have not gone away. With Na Krutci open both ways
again, he says, the tailback on the approach to Prague still regularly reaches Nebušice and
Pučálka, as it did in September — suggesting the cause is the volume of cars entering the city
rather than the one-way street. The borough says it will evaluate traffic on surrounding roads for
September and October, when the restriction applied, and for November, when it did not.

The leadership will also meet the mayors of the neighbouring municipalities before the end of the
year. They boycotted the previous meeting.

## Why it matters beyond Vokovice

The one-way was always temporary, and it has already ended. What remains open is the rule. The
Municipal Court did not rule on whether the traffic regime was right or wrong, Hrubeš said after
the judgment; it ruled on how it was justified and consulted. A Supreme Administrative Court
judgment on those points would bind more than one street in Prague 6: it would set the standard
any Prague district has to meet when it closes a rat-run against drivers who live outside it —
and the evidence it must have before it starts.
