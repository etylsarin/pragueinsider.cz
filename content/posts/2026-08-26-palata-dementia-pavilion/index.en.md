Prague has formally launched the city's first mental health centre for seniors and confirmed that
the new pavilion meant to extend it — Palata II, with 73 places — is structurally complete and due
to open in April 2027. City representatives and Domov Palata, which will run both services,
presented them to journalists on 24 August, according to a ČTK report carried by archiweb.

The centre itself has been running since March as a pilot project, in rented premises on
Starokošířská street in Prague 5. It offers free help to seniors with mental illness — dementia in
particular — as well as to their families and carers. The team is a mix of psychiatrist,
geriatrician and social workers, and can work with people directly in their own homes. It currently
looks after roughly 45 clients.

## Two services, one building, in that order

The sequencing here is worth noting, because it inverts the usual pattern. Prague has run the
service for five months in rented rooms and is now building the premises around it, rather than
completing a building and then finding a use.

Deputy Mayor Alexandra Udženija (ODS) framed the centre as the first tier of a graded system.

> "Our aim is for seniors to be able to live at home as long as possible, in familiar surroundings
> and among their loved ones. And if that is no longer possible, we must offer them dignified, safe
> and high-quality residential services."
>
> — Alexandra Udženija, Deputy Mayor of Prague (ODS)

Per Udženija, seniors and their relatives can approach the centre for information alone, and the
team will then help them find care matching their needs.

## What Palata II contains

The new pavilion goes up on the grounds of the Domov Palata complex, which already contains Palata
I — a historical building operating a special regime for clients with dementia, among them people
with disabilities. Palata II adds 73 places.

The internal split is four wards for 60 clients in the second stage of dementia, plus two
specialised "Oáza" wards for a further 13 people in the most severe phase of the illness. Udženija
says the pavilion is laid out to give clients privacy and to resemble a domestic environment as far
as possible; consulting rooms and adapted outdoor spaces are part of it.

The rough construction is already finished. Udženija put completion in the spring of next year, with
April the expected date.

Once the pavilion is done, part of the mental health centre moves into Palata II, where the
consulting rooms and other back-of-house facilities for it will sit. That is the point at which the
two services stop being separate: support for seniors who can stay at home, and residential care for
those who no longer manage at home, run from the same address.

## The expansion behind it

Prague is preparing a further extension of these services. The city has picked another building in
Prague 5 which it wants to transfer under Domov Palata and convert into a complete mental health
centre for seniors. Details will be presented later, as preparation progresses — so the site has not
been named.

The capacity figures Udženija gave put the pavilion in context. Since 2024 Prague has increased
capacity in its homes for seniors by 147 places; a further 193 places are currently under
construction, and realisation is being prepared for roughly 300 more. In total, per Udženija, the
city has secured or is preparing more than 600 new places in homes for seniors.

Palata II's 73 places are therefore around 38 per cent of what is currently on site across the city,
and around an eighth of the whole programme. Udženija tied the programme to the gradual ageing of
the population, which she said is driving up the need for both capacity and services.

All the figures above are the city's own, presented at its own press conference; ČTK's report does
not carry an independent check on them, and neither do we. What is verifiable on the ground is the
one thing that is already built: a completed rough structure in Košíře with a stated opening date
eight months out.
