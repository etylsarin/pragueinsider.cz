The three glass boxes that Karel Prager and Jiří Kadeřábek set down beside the Emauzy monastery in
1973 are about to be taken apart. Not demolished — dismantled, item by item, with an inventory kept
of what comes out. The reconstruction of the complex at Vyšehradská 51, home to the city's planning
institute IPR and its public-facing gallery CAMP, began at the end of June and is scheduled to run
three years at a cost of 1.4 billion crowns, funded by Prague City Hall.

## The building has been waiting half a century

Prager's complex has never had a comprehensive renovation. What is being restored is not simply the
fabric but the original architectural proposition: modern offices, exhibition halls, and — the part
the city lost — publicly accessible terraces and a deliberate openness toward the street. Fifty
years of ad-hoc adaptation buried most of that.

The design work is split across four teams. The reconstruction concept is led by Professor Miloslav
Pavlík, who worked with Prager himself. A ČVUT expert team handles the envelope and building
systems, IXA studio the spatial arrangement, and Land05 the landscape.

## Taking a building apart is a design problem

The more unusual half of the project is procedural. Before anything came out, a pre-reconstruction
audit by ČVUT mapped what the building contains that can be recovered: steel elements, concrete,
brick, glass. The institute has partnered with Cyrkl, a Czech company that trades in reclaimed
construction material, to find those components a second life rather than a skip.

> "In the buildings, bare partitions and empty rooms will remain. Then comes the dismantling of the
> elements."
>
> — Ondřej Boháč, director of IPR Praha

Recovered fittings are being offered onward to city institutions, non-profits and the public.

> "The goal of circular reconstruction isn't merely minimising waste. We want to keep materials and
> equipment in circulation, and find new applications for them."
>
> — Martina Škvorová, Cyrkl

The distinction matters. Construction and demolition waste is the largest waste stream in the
country by mass, and the standard response — crushing concrete into road aggregate — is technically
recycling while destroying most of the value. Keeping a steel frame as a steel frame is a different
and much harder thing to organise, which is why almost nobody does it. That a public institution is
attempting it on its own headquarters, at this scale, is the part of this project other clients
will be watching.

## Where CAMP goes meanwhile

The architecture centre closed its Vyšehradská home with a party on 25 June, capping a farewell
programme titled *Stavba povolena* that ran through the month. It reopens in autumn at Pobřežní 20a
in Karlín, in a former ship repair facility — a temporary address for the next three years, and a
reasonably fitting one for an institution spending that time thinking about what buildings are made
of.
