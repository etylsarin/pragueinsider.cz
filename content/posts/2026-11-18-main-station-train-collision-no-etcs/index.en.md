Two České dráhy passenger trains collided head-on at Prague's main station at around 7:20 on the
morning of Tuesday 17 November. According to the latest figures from the emergency services
reported by Zdopravy.cz, 46 people were injured, four of them seriously; both drivers are among
them. Traffic through part of the Prague rail node was halted for about two hours and was back to
full operation at 12:38.

The trains met on the railway bridge that carries the tracks over Seifertova street at the U Bulhara
junction, just outside the station throat. One was Os 9409, a CityElefant unit leaving Prague for
Milovice; the other Os 9507, a RegioFox inbound from Mladá Boleslav.

## What the inspectorate says so far

The Drážní inspekce, the state rail accident investigator, sent three inspectors. Its preliminary
view, as reported by Zdopravy.cz, is that the probable cause was a signal passed at danger, and that
the damage will exceed 16 million korunas. Police criminal investigators are also on site, working
with the inspectors to establish, in the police's words, the causes and circumstances and above all
who bears responsibility.

Zdopravy.cz reports that footage from a camera monitoring traffic at the station, which has
circulated online, shows the Milovice train departing past a signal that prohibited movement. That
is the footage and the inspectorate's first assessment; the investigation is not finished, and
nothing here is a finding of fault.

The Prague fire service spokesman Miroslav Řezáč said the collision was not destructive, and that
the consequences were limited partly because trains still run relatively slowly at that point. The
Prague emergency medical service declared its major-incident plan:

> We are recording several seriously injured people who were at risk of failure of basic vital
> functions.
>
> — Karel Kirs, spokesman for the Prague emergency medical service, to ČTK

## The missing system

The single line in the report that matters beyond Tuesday is this one: Praha hlavní nádraží is not
equipped with ETCS, the European train control system, which Zdopravy.cz says would most likely have
prevented an incident of this kind. (By way of context, not from the report: the onboard and
trackside parts of ETCS together supervise a train against the movement it is authorised to make,
which a lineside signal alone does not.)

Zdopravy.cz published alongside its report a map of ETCS coverage across the Prague rail node, with
the parts that have none marked in grey. The report gives no date for when the main station will be
covered, and we do not either.

## The disruption and the operator

At first nothing could run from the main station towards the Vítkov tunnels, though trains to the
Vinohrady tunnels kept moving. Shortly after nine, departures resumed from four platforms; by the
operator's mid-morning update, Libeň could be reached on two tracks and Balabenka and Vysočany on
one, departures were limited to tracks 13 to 20, and R10 trains between Prague and Hradec Králové
were starting and ending at Praha-Vysočany. Dozens of regional and long-distance trains were
diverted to, or turned at, Holešovice, Libeň and Vysočany, and ČD tickets were accepted on selected
city transport lines.

Lenka Hamplová, head of ČD, apologised on X and promised full cooperation with the
investigation and "adequate measures, should a fault on our side be proven". The operator has also
promised compensation for injury and for damage to property to passengers of both trains.

What would make this worth returning to is the inspectorate's final report — and whether the answer
to a signal passed at danger at the main station is a disciplinary one or an infrastructural
one.
