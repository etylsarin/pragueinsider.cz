Prague 6 has chosen the Prague studio Atelier bod architekti to redesign Kulturní centrum Kaštan,
the protected building on Bělohorská street in Břevnov. Expats.cz reported on 16 November, citing the district's announcement that
it had approved the results of the architectural competition, that the winning design was picked
from 44 entries submitted by studios across the Czech Republic. A professional jury had narrowed the
field to nine finalists in February.

The decision moves Kaštan from intention to a named architect and a concrete brief, which is the
point at which a district project starts to have a shape people can argue with.

## What the design does

According to Expats.cz, the winning proposal has three parts. The existing Baroque-Classical building
is renovated. Beside it go two new structures: one containing a cultural hall for 150 to 200
visitors, the other a garden café with facilities for markets and community events. The design also
takes in the park and public space between Bělohorská and Patočkova streets.

That loss is the building's history. Kaštan was originally an inn before it became a cultural venue.
In 1955 part of it was demolished when Bělohorská was widened, and what remained was declared a
cultural monument in 1962. The site lies close to Břevnov Monastery. Today the centre has a theatre
hall, an art education studio, a café and the Museum of Popular Music, and it is run by the civic
association Unijazz.

## What the district says

Expats.cz quotes the deputy mayor, Petr Prokop, in English:

> The winning proposal best combines urban planning with a sensitive approach to the unique
> surroundings of Břevnov.
>
> — Petr Prokop, deputy mayor of Prague 6, as quoted by Expats.cz

He added, the outlet reports, that the aim is a new cultural and social centre for the neighbourhood
at a scale suited to the area. The mayor, Jakub Stárek, called the reconstruction one of the
district's major long-term projects:

> We want to preserve the historic character of the protected building … creating a modern and
> functional cultural facility for the 21st century.
>
> — Jakub Stárek, mayor of Prague 6, as quoted by Expats.cz

Both quotations are Expats.cz's English renderings; the Czech originals are in the district's
announcement, which we were unable to retrieve.

## What has not been decided

No construction date has been announced, and no cost is given. The next step, Expats.cz reports, is
negotiation with Atelier bod over the full project documentation, and when building starts depends
on how that goes. The district plans a public exhibition of all the competition entries, the winner
and the other finalists alike; Expats.cz dates it only to "the autumn".

Two questions follow from the brief rather than from anything the district has said. A hall of 150
to 200 seats next to a listed building near the monastery is a real addition of volume in a
sensitive place, and the exhibition will be the first chance to see how much of it is visible from
Bělohorská. And the venue's present operator, Unijazz, does not appear in what Expats.cz reports
about the centre's future — which leaves open, on the published record, who will run the larger
centre once it exists.
