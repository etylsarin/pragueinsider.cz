A wall on the Kampa riverbank, opposite Střelecký ostrov, now carries a 70-metre mosaic by the
illustrator Petr Sís. *Sametový most* — the Velvet Bridge — was unveiled on Tuesday 17 November, the
national holiday, with the artist present, Expats.cz reported the next day. It is made of 6,300
hand-painted tiles and is about 3.4 metres high.

The date is the same one on which the project was launched, seven years earlier. On 17 November 2019,
the thirtieth anniversary of the Velvet Revolution, Sís put up two trial panels on the same wall — one
of Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, one of Václav Havel — and Prague 1 announced that the whole work would be
installed in the spring of 2020. ČT24 gave May 2020 as the completion date.

## What is on the wall

The composition is a playful version of Charles Bridge, as ČT24 and Prague 1 described it in 2019:
the history of Czechoslovakia from 1918 to 1989 is told in the arches, twelve panels in all, in a
style close to a comic strip. According to Expats.cz the finished arches include Masaryk and Havel,
the interwar First Republic, the Munich Agreement, the trains of Nicholas Winton's rescue of children
and the events of 1968, alongside everyday images — communist-era Pioneers, rock music and the Lennon
Wall. Sís drew on imagery from his own children's books. The tiles are Portuguese, according to ČT24.

## How it was set up

The wall belongs to Prague 1, according to ČT24, and had not been used for anything before. Prague 1
described it in 2019 as dilapidated, regularly covered in graffiti and in need of complete
reconstruction before the mosaic could go on it. The district council approved the installation in
November 2019.

The work is a gift to the city rather than a city commission. Prague 1 named its partners as the
district itself, Amnesty International Czech Republic, the Václav Havel Library and the DOX Centre for
Contemporary Art, with the idea coming from Petr Šimůnek, editor-in-chief of Forbes, and Bill
Shipsey, founder of Art for Amnesty. About three quarters of the cost was to come from private donors
and a quarter from a public collection; ČT24 reported a symbolic contribution of 89 crowns per donor
towards the tiles. Neither 2019 source gave a total.

Pavel Čižinský, then mayor of Prague 1, explained the choice of site at the launch:

> It is symbolic that from this wall we can almost see the windows of Václav Havel's former flat.
>
> — Pavel Čižinský, mayor of Prague 1, in 2019 (our translation from the Prague 1 release)

Sís lived in Prague until he was 33 before moving to New York, according to
Expats.cz. He has written around 30 children's books, illustrated about 60, and received the Hans
Christian Andersen Award; in 2023 President Petr Pavel gave him the Medal of Merit.

## What is missing

The gap between spring 2020 and November 2026 is the part of this story the sources do not tell. The
Expats.cz report on the unveiling does not mention the delay, the wall's reconstruction, the final
cost or who now looks after the work. Czech reports of the unveiling that we found could not be reached from our desk, so this
account of the finished work rests on that one outlet, with the background from 2019.

## Reading it

A permanent artwork of this size on a public riverside wall is a change to one of the most-walked
stretches of the Vltava, on a wall the district described in 2019 as a graffiti problem. It is also a reminder
of how long a gift can take: a project announced as six months from completion took close to seven
years, and the public record of why is, for now, thin.
