The annual public transport pass in Prague will go from 3,650 to 4,750 crowns next spring. The
mayor-designate, Petr Hlaváček (STAN), confirmed the figure in an interview with Novinky.cz, as
reported by Zdopravy.cz on 16 November, and two days later the incoming deputy mayors for transport
and finance repeated it after the members of the new city council were presented. Hlaváček first
floated the figure after the October election; it is now the stated position of the whole STAN–Spolu–
Praha sobě coalition, and it comes with a second, vaguer commitment: parking is to cost more too.

## The pass goes back to 2015

The rise is 1,100 crowns, roughly 30 per cent. It is also a return rather than an invention: the
pass cost 4,750 crowns until the end of June 2015, when the city leadership under mayor Adriana
Krnáčová (ANO) cut it by exactly that amount, and 3,650 has not moved since. Tomáš Portlík (ODS),
the incoming deputy mayor for finance, told ČTK that the coalition agrees on going back to the 2015
price. Hlaváček said the price should afterwards follow inflation. Neither the start date of the new
tariff nor the rules for later rises have been set, according to Zdopravy.cz.

Adam Scheinherr (Praha sobě), nominated as deputy mayor for transport, said a higher pass was not his
personal priority, but that if the city wants more comfort for passengers and more transport
construction, prices have to rise at least with inflation:

> Today we have an annual pass for 3,650 crowns, the cheapest of all 14 regional cities. It is
> cheaper than 26 years ago, when it cost 3,800.
>
> — Adam Scheinherr, deputy mayor-designate for transport, to ČTK

The arithmetic behind it is in the PID transport plan to 2030, which the assembly approved in June:
in 2025 fares covered 17.5 per cent of the cost of public transport, against a target of 25 per cent
by 2030, and the plan recommends revising the prices of passes and the system of discounts.
Hlaváček put the city's annual subsidy to the transit company DPP at more than 20 billion crowns and
said it would remain large after the rise. Single tickets already went up in January; the basic
30-minute ticket is 39 crowns, or 36 in the PID Lítačka app. Whether they change again is still to be
negotiated.

## Parking: a direction, not a proposal

The last coalition tried the same pairing and failed. The Pirates made a higher pass conditional on
higher parking charges; ODS opposed the parking reform; in the end only single tickets rose. The new
coalition says it will raise both, and adjust the paid parking zones, but it has no worked-out scheme.
Scheinherr wants experts to draw one up, then have it discussed in the city's parking commission and
among the coalition partners:

> So I don't have a concrete proposal right now.
>
> — Adam Scheinherr, to ČTK

Portlík was more specific about what one partner wants. STAN, he said, wants to double the price of
resident parking and to let people buy a card valid in zones across the whole city — outside the
centre, in districts other than their own. That is STAN's position as Portlík described it; Zdopravy's
own headline presents the doubling as the coalition's, but neither article reports the partners
agreeing to it. Scheinherr would like both the fare and the parking changes approved in the first half
of 2027.

## The centre, the bridge and a coordinator

On traffic in the historic core, the agreement is procedural. Scheinherr said the coalition has agreed
that the city's planning institute should propose measures for the Prague heritage reserve and that
they should then be introduced step by step; what they will be depends on the study. The outgoing
coalition had wanted to charge for entry to Smetanovo nábřeží and Malá Strana, and dropped it. Neither
article mentions the toll inside the city ring that Hlaváček raised after the October election.

Scheinherr also wants a transport coordinator: a post that would schedule construction not only for
its immediate effect on traffic but against the city's long-term plans, using information systems
that let city and private firms work together.

The Libeň bridge is the one place where he offered a deadline of sorts. Work on it stopped after the
competition authority cancelled the reconstruction contract. The coalition has agreed that the
priority is to finish the part of the bridge complex already under construction so trams can run
again, and to find the fastest solution for the river crossing itself:

> I think that during next year we will have that solution, so that the final state is definitely
> earlier than 2034, as is now planned.
>
> — Adam Scheinherr, to ČTK

## Reading it

Of everything set out this week, only the pass has a figure, a season and the backing of all three
partners. Parking — the issue on which the previous coalition broke — has a target date but no
content, and the content on offer belongs to one party. If the 2027 deadline is to hold, the expert
proposal Scheinherr describes has to exist within months, and that is where the coalition's agreement
will first be tested.
