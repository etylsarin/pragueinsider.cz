Prague's city councillors approved a new primary school at Západní město in Prague 13 as a new
investment action on 31 August. It is to hold 1,152 pupils and cost roughly CZK 1.03 billion
excluding VAT, of which private investors in the area contribute CZK 112 million. The city budget
covers the rest across 2027 to 2029.

The campus is to include classrooms, gymnasiums and further sports facilities, a central kitchen
with a dining hall, parking and transport access, in buildings the city hall says will be
barrier-free and energy-efficient.

## The land and the paperwork came before the money

What distinguishes this from an announcement is how far the preparation has already run. According
to the city hall the project is at an advanced stage: the design documentation is complete, and the
city holds both a final permission for the scheme and the documentation for construction. What is
missing is a start date — when building could begin is not yet clear.

Prague 13 and the city prepared the project together with an investor in the area. Under a signed
planning agreement, the district and the city secured the land for the building including its
connection to technical infrastructure, the design documentation and the necessary permits, while
the private investors put in CZK 112 million.

That is worth reading carefully. The developer money is roughly eleven per cent of the cost. The
public side supplied the site, the utility connections and the entire consent process, which are
the parts of a school project that take years rather than money. On those proportions the planning
agreement is less a contribution to construction than a settlement of the obligation attached to
building housing on the land next door.

## A primary school now, possibly a secondary school later

Antonín Klecanda (STAN), the city councillor for education, said the plan is chiefly a primary
school for now, though the city is also considering part of it as a secondary school. Its form and
scale may change according to how the area develops and what capacity the school system needs.

That flexibility matters more here than it would elsewhere. Districts found and run primary and
nursery schools in Prague; the city hall founds secondary, higher vocational, special, language and
basic art schools. A campus that could take a secondary school later is one where the city is
building for its own future obligation as well as the district's present one.

## Why Stodůlky

The city hall counts Prague 13 among the fast-growing parts of the capital. Flats have been going up
in Stodůlky since 2008 and construction is to continue under the new spatial plan; the population
has risen with it, and so has demand for nursery, primary and secondary places.

The shortage of school capacity is a long-running problem across Prague, in the outer districts and
the wider centre alike, tied to housing construction and a growing population putting pressure on
school facilities. Alongside new buildings, the city is dealing with it by extending existing
schools with annexes and modernising their grounds.

Read against the school Prague 14 broke ground on at Jahodnice four days earlier, one thing is
consistent: the city hall is in the room for school projects the districts formally own, because
neither the land nor the building is within a district's means alone. At Západní město the
developer line is CZK 112 million against CZK 1.03 billion — useful, and nowhere near enough on its
own.
