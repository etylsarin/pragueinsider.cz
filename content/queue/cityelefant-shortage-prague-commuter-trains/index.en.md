Commuters on České dráhy services around Prague are travelling in half-length trains. The operator
has an unusually high number of CityElefant electric units out of service and is running single
units on services that normally take two coupled together.

The numbers came from ČD spokeswoman Vanda Rajnochová, who told Zdopravy.cz on the morning of
9 September that 24 of the 71 CityElefant units the operator runs in Prague and Central Bohemia were
out of service. Operational need is 57. That leaves the fleet roughly ten trains short of what the
timetable requires, and the shortfall lands on lines S1, S9 and partly S7, with the exact scope
shifting day to day.

The regional politician who orders those services put it more bluntly. Petr Borecký, the Central
Bohemian councillor for public transport, said 22 CityElefants and three RegioPanters had been out
of service the previous day, and that enough vehicles could not be returned to traffic even for the
Wednesday morning peak.

> Takže asi budete stát na jedné noze a ještě ne na té své.
>
> — Petr Borecký, Central Bohemian councillor for public transport

České dráhy will be fined, according to Borecký. The operator said on X that it had managed to keep
every service running, that many trains were formed of one unit rather than the usual two and
therefore had lower capacity, and that it was working intensively to return vehicles to service —
adding that the restrictions may persist for days. On affected trains, first-class compartments are
available without a surcharge, and the region asked passengers not to take their frustration out on
conductors, who cannot influence train length.

## The causes, and the one with a date on it

Rajnochová listed several reasons at once: technical faults, scheduled maintenance, damage from
incidents and collisions with animals, and the ongoing installation of the European train protection
system ETCS on part of the fleet — above all on the units intended for the corridor lines around
Prague. The faults themselves are mundane: door repairs, air conditioning.

It is the ETCS work that has a deadline attached, and the deadline was confirmed the following day.
Správa železnic told operators and ordering authorities in a bulk message that from 13 December no
train will be admitted between Prague and České Budějovice without the onboard part of the European
signalling system. Exclusive ETCS operation extends over a further 157 kilometres, between
Praha-Zahradní Město and České Budějovice, plus the link between Uhříněves and Malešice. The
announcement was framed as definitive, ending speculation that the start might be put off again;
historic trains without ETCS will get on only by special exemption applied for well in advance.

That is a hard date for a fleet that has to be retrofitted one unit at a time, and the units come
out of traffic to be fitted. The shortage is, in part, the price of the deadline being kept.

## What the system is actually doing

The reliability case for exclusive operation has improved sharply. Správa železnic's figures put the
share of trips — the interruptions of the connection that force a train to stop — at 0.27 percent in
the first half of this year, with 0.22 percent in July and 0.33 percent in August. Last year the
full-year figure was 0.64 percent, and in the first months of exclusive operation it was far worse,
running to dozens of cases a day.

Tomáš Konopáč, who heads ERTMS strategy and technology at Správa železnic, attributed the
improvement to a set of regime measures across the network — scheduled maintenance of the
transmission system set long in advance — and to better functioning of the onboard equipment,
particularly the communication antennas and odometry.

The December change also removes hardware from the lineside: all speed boards and distant speed
boards for speeds above 100 km/h go, roughly 450 of them, because above that speed the driver will
get the permitted speed only through ETCS. Boards for 100 km/h and below stay, precisely so drivers
have something to read when ETCS drops out.

## This has happened before, for the same reasons

PID spokesman Filip Drápal said the organiser was in close contact with the operator and believed it
was doing everything it could to raise the number of serviceable units.

> Situace nás samozřejmě vůbec netěší.
>
> — Filip Drápal, spokesman, PID

Prague is not seeing this for the first time. In December 2024, only 51 of the same 71 units were
serviceable — nine below the minimum the ordering authorities considered necessary — and the cause
then was the same combination of technical faults, maintenance and ETCS installation. Two shortages
two years apart, produced by the same three things, describe a fleet with no spare capacity to lose.
The fine is for the symptom. The reserve is the problem.
