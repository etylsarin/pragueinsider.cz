Prague's own developer has settled who will draw the first housing blocks at Nové Dvory. Pražská
developerská společnost, the city magistrate's in-house organisation for housing projects, announced
the winners of two architectural competitions for the quarter it intends to build around the future
metro D station: re:architekti took one, Apropos Architects the other. Together the blocks are to
carry roughly a thousand flats. The press release went out on 17 September and ČTK carried the
result on 18 September, quoting the company's media representative Markéta Miková.

The arithmetic in the two sources does not line up neatly and is worth stating plainly. The press
release describes two competitions, numbered Nové Dvory 5 and Nové Dvory 6. ČTK counts three blocks
around the station, one designed by re:architekti and two by Apropos Architects. Read together, the
second competition covers more than one block.

## What the jury bought

The competitions were restricted ones, run under Czech procurement law and the Chamber of
Architects' competition rules, with CCEA MOBA as organiser. Thirty studios qualified — fifteen for
each — and proposals were due on 28 August. Every qualified entrant that submitted was paid 115,000
crowns, and each competition set aside roughly three million crowns for prizes and compensation.
The jury sat in early September and judged the entries anonymously: independent architects with
representatives of the city, of Prague 4 and of PDS, and specialists in transport, economics, energy
and landscape architecture. The chair was the architect Šimon Vojtík.

> In the end what decided it for both winners was above all the ability to combine a strong
> architectural concept with a rational solution appropriate to municipal rental housing.
>
> — Šimon Vojtík, chair of the jury

The jury's reading of the re:architekti block is that it answers two things at once: the panel
housing estate next door and the planned centre of Nové Dvory, working with corners, changes of
height, gardens and views to produce a range of living conditions rather than one. Apropos
Architects were credited with alternating taller and lower sections so that the development reads at
several scales, and with a courtyard that combines a through route on foot, publicly accessible
pieces and a quieter residential part.

Petr Urbánek, the director of PDS, tied both back to the territorial study the magistrate had drawn
up for the area.

> They fulfil what is the basis of the Nové Dvory urban study, that is, they have a legible block
> structure, a lively ground floor around the future metro D station and a connection to public
> spaces. All of that makes a full-fledged city quarter, one that will be good to live in.
>
> — Petr Urbánek, director of Pražská developerská společnost

## The two buildings on top of the station

Separately from the competitions, PDS is preparing the projects for two buildings that will stand
directly above the metro station itself, on the corner of Durychova and Libušská. Besides the
entrances to the metro, the city intends to put housing, civic amenities, offices and a social and
cultural hall inside them.

That is the part that will decide whether this works as a piece of city rather than as a housing
estate with a station under it. A block with a lively ground floor is easy to draw and hard to let;
a building that contains the metro entrance has a reason for people to walk through it every day.

## The dates do not match the drawings

Nové Dvory station is part of metro D, which will link Náměstí Míru with Písnice and whose
construction started in 2022. On the current schedule the line should begin running between Pankrác
and Nové Dvory in the second half of 2032, with automatic trains; the city also plans, further out,
to extend it from Náměstí Míru to Náměstí Republiky.

So the architects have been chosen for blocks whose central argument — a metro station at the
doorstep — is six years away. PDS will now open a negotiated procedure without publication with the
two winners and sign contracts for the project documentation. On a city timetable that is not
premature: design documentation, permits and procurement will use most of the interval. It does mean
the first residents of municipal rental flats at Nové Dvory may arrive before the trains do.
