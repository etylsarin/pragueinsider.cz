The company building Smíchov City has signed a donation agreement with the City of Prague worth 120
million crowns, and the city assembly approved accepting it last week. Part of the money goes to
the new primary school on the former railway land, ZŠ Smíchov City. Prague 5, which announced the
agreement on 15 September, describes it as the conclusion of a long-running arrangement between the
city, the district and the developer.

The donor is Hrotano Czech, s. r. o. The reason the money goes to the city rather than to the
district is the more interesting half of the story.

## Why the district gave the school away

ZŠ Smíchov City has been in preparation since 2016. It is designed for 580 children and Prague 5
puts the cost at roughly 1.16 billion crowns. In July the district completed the transfer of the
project to the city, on the plain ground that building it exceeds what the district's finances can
do. A 1.16-billion school is not a municipal-district scale of investment, and Prague 5 stopped
pretending otherwise.

> We managed to negotiate that the city will deliver the new educational facility as well as the
> transport infrastructure, which is why the contribution flows directly to it.
>
> — Zdeněk Doležal (ODS), Prague 5 councillor for spatial development

Mayor Lukáš Herold (ODS) says the school could be operating by 2030. The visualisations the
district published are credited to Office Ou & Inostudio and Atelier M1 architekti.

> Building the primary school in Smíchov City is one of the most important investments in Prague
> 5's future. The new quarter will bring thousands of new residents to Smíchov and it could not do
> without the corresponding school infrastructure.
>
> — Lukáš Herold (ODS), mayor of Prague 5

## What else the investor is committed to

Beyond the donation, Hrotano Czech is to build at its own cost two large parks and a boulevard with
shops and services. That is the part of a masterplanned quarter that usually gets promised and then
argued about, so the specifics of the last item matter more than the headline sum.

On the final block, by the Radlická kulturní sportovna, the investor is to build a public space and
hand it to the district. That space is to go through an architectural competition by 31 December
2027 at the latest, and for five years after completion the investor is to manage and maintain it
at its own expense.

A dated competition obligation and a five-year maintenance obligation are the two things that
distinguish a public space a developer builds from one a developer draws. Both are in this
agreement, with a deadline attached to the first.

## The arithmetic worth holding onto

The donation is 120 million against a school costing about 1.16 billion. It covers roughly a tenth
of the building, and the district's own wording is that the city will use part of the gift on the
school — the agreement also names transport infrastructure in Prague 5 as a purpose. So this is a
contribution to the cost of the district Smíchov City is generating, not a payment for the school.

Prague 5's headline calls the sum "almost 120 million"; the body of its announcement gives the
donation as 120 million crowns. We have used the figure from the text.

The scale on the other side of the ledger is what gives the number its meaning. Thousands of
residents are moving into a quarter built on land that had no school of its own, and the school
that is meant to serve them is still, in September 2026, a project with a design, a transferred
client and a first tranche of private money — not a building site.
